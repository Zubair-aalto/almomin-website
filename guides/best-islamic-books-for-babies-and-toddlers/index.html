The best Islamic books for babies and toddlers (0–3)

It’s never too early to read with your child. Here’s how to choose Islamic books that match each stage from birth to age three, and the titles parents in Finland love most.

0–6 months: high contrast and soft pages

Newborns see bold black-and-white shapes best, and they explore everything with their mouths. Choose soft cloth books with high-contrast pictures and crinkly pages. Read the words softly, say Bismillah together, and let your baby touch and hold the book.

6–18 months: touch, mirrors and first words

Babies now love textures, faces and repetition. Touch-and-feel and mirror board books keep little hands busy while you introduce simple ideas: Allah made the sea, the sun and me; I am loved; I am kind. Short, rhythmic lines that you repeat every day build both language and faith.

18 months–3 years: flaps, routines and family

Toddlers love lifting flaps and finding surprises, and they learn through routines. This is a lovely age for books about Salah, everyday Sunnah sayings and family. Read them at the same time each day, for example after Maghrib or before bed, and let your child “find” the answers.

Tips for reading with little ones

  • Little and often. Two minutes a few times a day beats one long session.
  • Use your own language. If you speak Urdu, Arabic, Somali or Finnish at home, tell the story in that language while pointing at the pictures.
  • Make dua together. Turn each book into a moment of remembrance: “Who made the trees? Allah!”
  • Keep books within reach. A low basket of board books invites toddlers to read on their own.
